\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Graph search and compressed construction}
Small-world routing~\cite{kleinberg2000smallworld} and search in growth-restricted metrics~\cite{karger2002growth} provide foundations for graph-based neighbor search. HNSW~\cite{malkov2020hnsw}, DiskANN/Vamana~\cite{jayaram2019diskann}, and NSG~\cite{fu2019nsg} turn graph navigation into practical indices. Monotonic proximity graphs~\cite{zhu2021monotonic} and error-bounded $\delta$-EMG~\cite{xiang2025emg} study conditions supporting search progress. LVQ~\cite{aguerrebere2023lvq} constructs graphs in a compressed space, and FAISS BinaryHNSW indexes binary vectors under Hamming distance~\cite{faissbinary}.

\paragraph{Indexing and query systems}
Graph-search surveys expose the distinct effects of initialization, candidate discovery, pruning, and query traversal~\cite{wang2021graphsurvey}. Flash uses compact codes and CPU-aware operations to accelerate construction~\cite{wang2025flash}; PiPNN organizes scalable construction around partitions and online pruning~\cite{dong2026pipnn}. SHG adds shortcuts to hierarchical navigation~\cite{gong2025shg}, PAG uses projections to reduce exact comparisons~\cite{lu2026pag}, and VSAG~\cite{zhong2025vsag} and USearch~\cite{usearch2024} address practical deployment.

\paragraph{Quantization and graph co-design}
NGT-QG stores neighboring codes contiguously for batched estimates~\cite{ngt}. SymphonyQG combines quantization, graph refinement, layout, and exact scoring during traversal~\cite{gou2025symphony}. Routing-guided product quantization trains a quantizer to preserve graph neighborhood and routing features~\cite{yue2024rpq}. QuIVer instead derives its codes from per-vector statistics without training.

\paragraph{Vector compression and exact refinement}
Product quantization~\cite{jegou2011pq}, optimized product quantization~\cite{ge2013opq}, and FAISS~\cite{johnson2021faiss} provide a broad range of compression choices, surveyed by Matsui et al.~\cite{matsui2018pqs}. PQ Fast Scan~\cite{andre2015pqfs} and Quick ADC~\cite{andre2017quickadc} show how code layout and SIMD lookup affect query cost. RaBitQ provides theoretically controlled distance estimation at one or more bits~\cite{gao2024rabitq,gao2025extended}. Lucene BBQ uses binary database codes, asymmetric query precision, and correction factors~\cite{trent2024bbq,veasey2024osq}, and OpenSearch exposes quantization as a storage choice~\cite{opensearch2024bq}.

\paragraph{Hashing and representation dependence}
Random-hyperplane rounding connects angular similarity to binary comparisons~\cite{goemans1995improved,charikar2002similarity}; locality-sensitive hashing~\cite{indyk1998lsh,datar2004pstable} and fast Johnson--Lindenstrauss transforms~\cite{ailon2009fastjlt} organize randomized retrieval. QuIVer instead uses coordinate sign bits directly, tying its behavior to the embedding's native geometry. InfoNCE~\cite{oord2018infonce} and the Gaussian structure it induces~\cite{betser2026infonce}, alignment and uniformity~\cite{wang2020understanding}, and the multimodal modality gap~\cite{liang2022modgap} characterize that geometry. Iterative quantization~\cite{gong2011itq} and anisotropic quantization~\cite{guo2020scann} adapt codes to search, and temperature control in contrastive training improves embedding compressibility~\cite{dinu2025temperature}; our study takes embeddings as given.
